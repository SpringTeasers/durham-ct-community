About Durham, Connecticut

Durham is a town in Middlesex County, Connecticut, in the south-central part of the state. It is a former farming village on the Coginchaug River, and it has kept the small-town character that history gave it.

Location

Where Durham is

Durham sits in Middlesex County in the south-central part of Connecticut, in the greater New Haven–Middlesex area.

The town shares Regional School District 13 with its neighbor Middlefield, and it is one of sixteen municipalities served by River Valley Transit across Middlesex County and parts of New London County.

History

A farming village on the Coginchaug

Durham's identity grew out of the Coginchaug River. Long before it was a commuter town, it was a farming village, and the river shaped both its layout and its economy. The Durham Town Green, in the center of town, is the historic heart of the community.

That same green became the home of the Durham Fair in 1916 — a connection the town still celebrates every September.

Government

How Durham is governed

Durham is governed by a Board of Selectmen of three members, elected to four-year terms. One of the three serves as First Selectman. The town also keeps the traditional New England Town Meeting form of local government.

Education

Schools

Durham's public schools are part of Regional School District 13, which serves both Durham and Middlefield. Frederick Brewster Elementary School and Frank Ward Strong School (middle school) are in Durham; John Lyman Elementary and Middlefield Memorial School are in Middlefield. The district also includes Coginchaug Regional High School and the Middlesex Transition Academy. District general info: 860-349-7200.

Places

Points of interest

  • Millers Pond State Park — a state park with an easy, flat trail circling the lake and rock outcrops used as fishing platforms.
  • Durham Town Green — the historic center of town.
  • Coginchaug River — the waterway that shaped Durham's farming history.
  • Pistapaug Mountain — a natural landmark within the town.
