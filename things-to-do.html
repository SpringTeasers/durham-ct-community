Things to Do in Durham

Durham is a town for people who like being outdoors and being part of a community. Here are the parks, landmarks, and events that residents point visitors toward first.

Outdoors

Parks and natural landmarks

Millers Pond State Park

Millers Pond State Park is a Connecticut state park in Durham. The trail around the lake is easy and flat, though it is rocky in places. Anglers will find rock outcrops that extend into the pond and are used as fishing platforms.

Difficulty
Easy, flat loop (some rocky sections)
Activity
Walking, fishing from the rock outcrops

Durham Town Green

The Durham Town Green is the historic center of town — and the original home of the Durham Fair, held here since 1916. If you want to understand Durham, start on the Green.

Coginchaug River

The Coginchaug River runs through Durham. It is the reason the town exists where it does — Durham grew up as a farming village on its banks.

Pistapaug Mountain

Pistapaug Mountain is one of Durham's natural landmarks, visible around town and a recognizable part of the local landscape.

Events

Events

The Durham Fair

The Durham Fair is the town's signature event. It takes over the Durham Town Green and the adjoining fairgrounds each September, and it has been 100% volunteer-run since 1916 — making it one of the largest volunteer-run agricultural fairs in North America.

106th Annual Durham Fair — September 24–27, 2026

Durham Fair official website ↗ (opens in a new tab)

How the fair grew

  • The fair was approved on June 16, 1916, and the Durham Fair Association formed on August 2, 1916.
  • It was incorporated in 1917 as the Durham Agricultural Fair Association, Inc.
  • It ran as a one-day event from 1916–1921, two days from 1922–1950, and three days from 1951. A fourth day (Thursday evening) was added in 2010.
  • There were no fairs in 1938 (a hurricane) or 1942–1944 (World War II). Hurricane Gloria hindered the 1985 opening.
  • The first fair drew about 2,000 people. A modern fair can draw 200,000+ with good weather.
  • The original 1916 parade ran down Main Street from Burckel's Corner (the intersection of Haddam Quarter Road and Main Street) and continued through 1924.

Contact the Fair
